§01 · OctoPrint plugin · MIT

The Snapmaker 2.1 we deserved

OctoBurn turns OctoPrint into the control room for the A350's two lasers, the 10W and the 2W infrared. You draw in LightBurn, press Start and stay with the machine. OctoBurn works out what LightBurn can't see: the focus, the shape of the surface, the object in the rotary and whether it is safe to fire.

§03 · The point

Attended, on purpose

OctoBurn is for attended burns. It takes the arithmetic out of laser work; it does not let you walk away from it.

The interlocks catch mistakes made while you are standing next to the machine. A door left open, a laser half seated, a frame that would drag the module across a clamp. They do not make unattended burning safe, and I would not try it.

What it changes is the loop between an idea and a mark on the material. Measure, see, draw, frame, burn, check, without typing a focus height or guessing a diameter.

§04 · What it does

What it does

It finds the focus

On the bed, on material of a given thickness, from a probe touch, or from the 10W's red dot where you last framed. The 2W infrared gets its focus from a probe touch and a focus ladder. It picks flat or rotary mode from what is plugged in.

FocusAuto mode

It follows the surface

Map a warped board or a curved lid with the probe or the red dot. The map goes into the printer, so every move LightBurn sends follows the surface, and mapped objects become obstacles the module keeps clear of.

Height mapsRed dot

It measures the round things

Plug in the rotary and the probe finds the axis, the diameter and the ends, then checks itself. Nothing is perfectly round or perfectly clamped, so it measures how the surface wobbles as it turns, up to 3 mm a turn, and follows it. Slight imperfections barely show.

RotaryProbe

It gives LightBurn eyes

The 10W module's own camera takes overlapping photos from 50 mm up. OctoBurn corrects the lens and stitches them in LightBurn's coordinates, so what you draw on the overlay is where the beam lands.

CameraLightBurn

It will not fire until it's safe

The module must be seated, the door shut and the laser armed with two clicks. Open the door and it disarms. The air purifier starts by itself and runs until the air is clear. All of it guards a burn you are watching.

InterlocksAir

LightBurn, live

OctoPrint answers as a GRBL laser over TCP, so jog, Frame, Start, Pause and Stop work from LightBurn while OctoPrint keeps the printer. Each laser has its own LightBurn devices. Framing uses the 10W's low beam or the 2W infrared's crosshair. A small script copies what OctoBurn measured into LightBurn's settings.

§05 · A burn, start to finish

Six steps, no arithmetic

  1. 01

    Put the work on the bed

    Then look at what else is there. The 10W module reaches 74 mm to the left of the beam.

  2. 02

    Check the mode

    You don't pick it. No rotary means flat; rotary plugged in means rotary, once the object is measured.

  3. 03

    Set the focus

    Bed plus thickness, a probe touch, or the red dot. The line underneath says the Z and where it came from.

  4. 04

    Frame in LightBurn

    OctoBurn checks the outline against anything tall it knows about before anything moves.

  5. 05

    Arm, Start, and stay with it

    Two clicks to arm, for 30 minutes at most. Press Start in LightBurn and watch it burn.

  6. 06

    Look at what you made

    After a flat burn OctoBurn takes a close-up photo and lists it with the time, energy and power.

§06 · The probe

One print, one pin

A BLTouch-style probe on a printed Quick Swap plate, with its pin on the 10W's beam axis, so a probe XY is a laser XY.

I measured the plate profile off Snapmaker's own 10W laser CAD and checked it with callipers against a real plate. An ESP32-C3 with a tiny OLED sits in a box on the front, drives the probe and reports every touch to OctoBurn over Wi-Fi. It swaps into the bay in place of the laser, which stays plugged in and parked, so the machine stays in laser mode.

One rule matters on the rotary. Home with the 10W seated, then swap to the probe without homing again. X homes about 12 mm differently with the probe on, and OctoBurn refuses to measure if you forget.

The STL, OpenSCAD source, wiring and ESPHome firmware are in the repository.

§07 · Install

Two minutes, one URL

From OctoPrint

Settings, Plugin Manager, Get More, then paste this into ... from URL:

https://github.com/chrisns/octoburn/releases/download/v0.1.0/octoprint_laserheightprobe-0.1.0-py3-none-any.whl

The close-up camera also wants numpy and opencv-python-headless in OctoPrint's Python. Everything else runs without them.

What you need

  • A Snapmaker 2.0 (written for the A350) with the Quick Swap kit and the 10W laser, the 2W infrared laser, or both
  • OctoPrint connected to it over USB
  • LightBurn, for drawing and burning
  • Optional: the probe head, the rotary module and the enclosure with its purifier

Hobby software, tested on one machine. Both lasers blind instantly and start fires. Wear goggles rated for your module's wavelength, keep the enclosure shut and stay with every burn.
